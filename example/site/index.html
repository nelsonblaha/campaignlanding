A proposal · Ridgeline · Example page

A library, but for tools.

A tile saw gets used for one weekend and then sits in a garage for nine years. This is a proposal for a lending library that buys the expensive things once, keeps them working, and lends them out for free.

The saw costs $380. You need it for one afternoon.

Most of what a household buys for a renovation is used once. Bought new, a starter set for one bathroom runs past a thousand dollars; borrowed, the same set is a form you fill in and a shelf someone else maintains.

A tool library is mostly shelving.

Eight hundred square feet, a roll-up door, and somewhere dry. That is the entire facilities requirement, which is why this can start in a corner of a building that already exists rather than waiting on one that does not.

“We had the room. Nobody had ever asked us for it.”

Lending a tool to someone who has never used one is how people get hurt.

So the checkout desk is also a two-minute demonstration, and anything with a blade comes with one. Members who already know how to use a thing teach the class for the thing they know.

  • Saturday sessions, one tool per session
  • Anything powered gets a demonstration at checkout
  • Members teach; nobody is paid for it in year one

Things come back broken. That is the job, not a failure of it.

A lending library that cannot fix what it lends becomes a pile of broken tools in eighteen months. The repair bench is budgeted first and staffed before the collection grows, which is the part most proposals leave out.

The honest version of a plan says what it is worst at. This one is worst at repair capacity, and it is the first thing that will break.

The proposal

A membership, not a rental counter.

Members pay what they can once a year, elect the people who run it, and can read the books. The tiers below are candidates for what those memberships could be, and members would decide the real ones.

Borrower

$0

Anyone with an address in town. Three items at a time, one week each.

Member

$40/year

A vote for the board, six items at a time, and first call on the workshop nights.

Supporter

$150/year

The same as a member. It costs more because someone has to cover the borrower who pays nothing.
